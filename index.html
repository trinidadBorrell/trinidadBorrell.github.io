About

From physics to AI Safety

I'm a physicist turned neuroscientist and deep learning researcher, currently a PhD candidate at the Paris Brain Institute and Forschungszentrum Jülich.

My trajectory runs from the mathematical foundations of particle physics, through the study of human and machine robustness to adversarial attacks, and into deep learning for clinical brain signals. This has meant working hands-on with transformer-based foundation models and graph variational autoencoders on EEG data from patients with disorders of consciousness. Additionally, I am interested in AI safety, particularly mechanistic interpretability, and was selected as a MARS V Fellow, where I collaborate with a small team on characterizing the persona space of large language models.

What Have EEG Foundation Models Actually Learned About the Conscious Brain?

Borrell, T., Belloli, L., Marraffini, G., Patil, K. R., Sitt, J. D., Raimondo, F.

The first systematic benchmark of EEG and time-series foundation models (TOTEM, LaBraM, NeuroLM, CBraMod, EEGPT, BIOT, SleepFM) for classifying patients with disorders of consciousness. EEG-pretrained and decoder-only models consistently outperform general-purpose time-series models.

The Variance Brain Foundation Models Forgot: Third-Order Statistics Predict Cognition Where Billion-Parameter Models Fail

Marraffini, G., Mahuas, G., Borrell, T., Shevchenko, V., Wassermann, D.

Shows that brain foundation models preserve a signal's variance but discard its third-order structure, and it is that third-order signal that predicts cognition where billion-parameter models fail.

Humans Can Correct Their Judgements Through Debate, but Weaker AI Models Can Not

Carro, M., Mester, D., Nieto, F., Stanchi, O., Bergman, G., Marraffini, G., Borrell, T., et al.

Compares how humans and language models revise their judgements through debate, finding that weaker AI models fail to correct themselves where humans do.
